# Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) Curiosity Rover Gale Crater Mineralogy and Sample Analysis at Mars (SAM)

## 1. Instrument Suite Architecture
The Sample Analysis at Mars (SAM) suite is the analytical centerpiece inside the chassis of the Curiosity rover at Gale Crater. SAM occupies nearly half of the rover’s science payload volume and contains three interconnected laboratory instruments:
1. **Quadrupole Mass Spectrometer (QMS):** Measures gases released from thermal volatilization of rock powder, with a mass-to-charge detection range of 1.5 to 535 Da and a sensitivity capable of sub-part-per-billion detection.
2. **Gas Chromatograph (GC):** Contains six analytical capillary chromatographic columns optimized for separating complex organic hydrocarbons, volatiles, and chiral enantiomers.
3. **Tunable Laser Spectrometer (TLS):** Measures precise isotopic ratios of carbon ($^{13}C/^{12}C$) and oxygen ($^{18}O/^{16}O$) in carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), and deuterium-to-hydrogen ($D/H$) ratios in water vapor ($H_2O$), alongside measuring atmospheric methane ($CH_4$) at background levels below 1 part per billion by volume (ppbv).

## 2. Sample Handling and Pyrolysis System
Drilled rock powder extracted by Curiosity’s percussive rotary drill (capable of penetrating up to 6.5 cm into rock) is sieved to particles $<150$ $\mu m$ and dropped into one of SAM’s 74 sample cups mounted on a rotating dual-ring carousel.
- **Pyrolysis Oven:** Samples are sealed in an oven and heated at a controlled ramp rate of 35°C/min up to a maximum temperature of 860°C under a helium carrier gas flow at 0.8 bar.
- **Wet Chemistry Cups:** Nine of the cups contain chemical derivatization reagents: seven cups with N-methyl-N-(tert-butyldimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamide (MTBSTFA) combined with dimethylformamide (DMF), and two cups with tetramethylammonium hydroxide (TMAH) for thermochemolysis of polar organic molecules like amino acids and carboxylic acids.

## 3. Detection of Indigenous Martian Organic Molecules
At the Sheepbed mudstone member within Yellowknife Bay, as well as the Cumberland drill site in the Murray Formation at Mount Sharp (Aeolis Mons), SAM made historic detections of indigenous Martian organic matter:
- **Chlorinated Hydrocarbons:** SAM detected chlorobenzene ($C_6H_5Cl$) at concentrations of 150 to 300 parts per billion (ppb), along with dichloroalkanes (1,2-dichloropropane, 1,2-dichlorobutane) and chloromethane. 
- **Origin of Chlorine:** The chlorination occurs during pyrolysis when trace indigenous organic molecules react with volatile oxychlorine species (magnesium, calcium, and sodium perchlorates, $ClO_4^-$) present in the Martian regolith at 0.5 to 1.0 weight percent.
- **Refractory Thiophenic and Aromatic Compounds:** High-temperature pyrolysis ($>600$°C) of the Mojave 2 and Confidence Hills mudstone samples released sulfur-bearing refractory heterocyclic organics, specifically thiophenes ($C_4H_4S$), benzothiophene, and alkylthiols, confirming that ancient lacustrine sediments preserved sulfurized organic macromolecules against cosmic radiation degradation for over 3 billion years.

## 4. Gale Crater Ancient Lacustrine Habitability
Data from SAM and the CheMin (Chemistry and Mineralogy X-ray diffraction) instrument proved that Gale Crater hosted a long-lived, neutral-to-alkaline, habitable freshwater lake system 3.8 to 3.1 billion years ago:
- **Clay Mineral Matrix:** CheMin identified 20 to 30 weight percent smectite phyllosilicates (nontronite and saponite clays), which formed under benign, low-salinity aqueous conditions with circum-neutral pH (pH 7 to 8).
- **Key Bioessential Elements:** Chemical analysis detected carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, and sulfur (CHNOPS) bound in bioavailable oxidized and reduced states, including nitrates ($NO_3^-$ at 70 to 1100 ppm) capable of supporting chemolithoautotrophic microbial metabolism.
- **Sulfate Abundance & Redox Stratification:** Evidence of stratified redox conditions (iron-rich hematite and magnetite interbedded with smectite clays) provided potential chemical energy gradients that could have fueled ancient anaerobic life.
