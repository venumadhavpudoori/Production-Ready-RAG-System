# Mars InSight Lander Seismic Experiment for Interior Structure (SEIS) and Internal Geophysics

## 1. Mission Objectives and Geodetic Station Setup
NASA’s InSight lander touched down on November 26, 2018, in Elysium Planitia on Mars. InSight’s primary scientific objective was to perform the first comprehensive seismic exploration of the internal structure, core radius, crustal thickness, and geothermal thermal state of a terrestrial planet other than Earth and the Moon.

## 2. SEIS Instrument Suite Architecture and Deployment
The Seismic Experiment for Interior Structure (SEIS), developed under French space agency (CNES) leadership with international contributions, is an ultra-sensitive six-axis seismometer placed directly onto the Martian regolith using InSight's robotic Instrument Deployment Arm (IDA).
- **VBB Sensors:** Three Very Broad Band (VBB) oblique pendulums housed inside a spherical titanium vacuum sphere ($<10^{-2}$ mbar) capable of measuring ground displacements down to $10^{-10}$ meters per second squared ($\sim 10^{-9}$ m/s² per $\sqrt{Hz}$ across 0.01 to 10 Hz).
- **SP Sensors:** Three Short Period (SP) micro-electro-mechanical system (MEMS) sensors measuring high-frequency vibrations from 0.1 to 50 Hz.
- **Environmental Shielding:** To isolate the seismometer from extreme Martian diurnal temperature fluctuations (which span over 80°C daily) and wind turbulence, SEIS was covered by the Wind and Thermal Shield (WTS)—a domed aerogel-insulated structure deployed directly over the instrument.
- **Auxiliary Payload Sensors (APSS):** Continuous pressure sensors (capable of 0.01 Pa resolution) and wind anemometers monitored the micro-barometric environment to decorrelate atmospheric pressure-induced ground tilt from true tectonic signals.

## 3. Martian Seismicity and Major Marsquake Events
Over its four-year operational lifetime (spanning more than 1,440 Martian sols), SEIS cataloged 1,319 confirmed marsquakes.
- **Seismic Frequency Bands:** Events were classified into two distinct families:
  1. High-frequency events (typically originating in the shallow lithosphere within 40° distance, showing strong seismic scattering similar to lunar quakes).
  2. Low-frequency broadband events (originating in the deeper mantle, displaying clear body wave arrivals for primary $P$-waves and secondary $S$-waves).
- **Monster Marsquake (Event S1222a):** On May 4, 2022 (Sol 1222), SEIS recorded event S1222a, a magnitude $M_W = 4.7 \pm 0.2$ quake. This event released more seismic energy than all other recorded marsquakes combined. S1222a reverberated for more than 10 hours and generated surface Rayleigh and Love waves that circumnavigated Mars multiple times.
- **Cerberus Fossae Tectonic Activity:** Multiple prominent low-frequency quakes (including S0173a and S0235b) localized to the Cerberus Fossae graben system (roughly 1,600 km east of InSight), demonstrating active extensional faulting and modern magma dynamics in the Martian lithosphere.

## 4. Internal Structure: Crust, Mantle, and Core Determinations
Receiver function analysis, seismic wave travel-time inversions, and surface wave dispersion from meteorite impacts and large marsquakes revealed the first precise structural model of the Martian interior:
- **Martian Crustal Thickness:** The crust beneath the Elysium Planitia landing site has a thickness of either $20 \pm 5$ km (under a two-layer crustal model) or $39 \pm 8$ km (under a three-layer model). Global average crustal thickness is estimated at $30$ to $72$ km. The upper crust consists of a highly porous, fractured layer approximately 8 to 10 km thick, caused by billions of years of impact bombardment.
- **Martian Mantle:** The Martian lithosphere extends to a depth of $400$ to $600$ km. The underlying mantle is chemically enriched in iron ($FeO$) compared to Earth's upper mantle, consisting primarily of olivine, pyroxenes, and garnet.
- **Martian Core:** Using core-reflected seismic shear waves ($ScS$ and $SKS$ phases), InSight determined that Mars has a liquid metallic core with a radius of $1,830 \pm 40$ km. This surprisingly large radius indicates a relatively low core density ($6.0$ to $6.3$ g/cm³), requiring that the liquid iron-nickel core contains a high fraction of light alloying elements, including roughly 10% to 15% sulfur by weight, along with smaller amounts of carbon, oxygen, and hydrogen.
